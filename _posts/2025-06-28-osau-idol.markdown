I’m not a clout chaser, but this opportunity was too fun to ignore. Twitter has an osu community tab that serves as an alternative to the horrible “For You” page, which will only intermittently show you osu content, forcing you to endlessly scroll as you sift through random garbage to find what you want. An FYP post will never end up on the osu community tab, but something on the osu community page may end up on the FYP page. With this in mind, it is easy to gain traction by posting on the osu community tab, so all of my posts were made there.

At first I had low expectations. Getting 1000 views on a YouTube video is quite impressive, but this isn’t the case for Twitter. I posted one of my YouTube osu meme edits and it gained 16k views and 300 likes. I felt like I discovered a powerful tool that no one else was using, as an average post in the osu community tab was a mediocre osu clip that somehow got thousands of impressions. Getting likes and comments on a social media post has a distinct good feeling to it, like getting praise for sharing a good idea or making a joke that evokes laughter.

>*High achievers are definitely lonely*

Why did I stop posting? I was quite secluded at this time, and spending time alone lets you fully immerse yourself in your own interests. This was an interest that no one else cared about. I had no one to discuss my plans with, and I knew it would annoy them if I did. The headspace you need to be in as a Twitter poster is a positive feedback loop of attention seeking. Your sole purpose is to spark discussion and farm engagements for a synthetic feeling of social interaction. It requires a lot of free time, so once you find other things to do, it breaks the pattern.

I posted some osu memes on my youtube channel and they gained thousands of views. No one else was posting osu memes on YouTube and there was a vast amount of them on Twitter and reddit that were being lost to time. YouTube is good at finding and resurfacing old videos. Downloading other people’s osu memes and reposting them without their permission is definitely immoral, but I wanted to achieve a certain goal. I was close to reaching YouTube monetization as I had a video from last year which gained about 3000 watch hours, and you need 4000 in total within a year to be monetized. I knew I would reach this goal if I stole memes and steadily uploaded them for the next two months. Most people do not care if their memes get reposted on a different platform, but some people are more passionate, more sensitive than others, and there is nothing wrong with that. However, it is your fault if you overlook this. I temporarily applied a membership feature on an upcoming video where only channel members could watch it. I was testing a new feature I got for reaching a channel milestone. I was called out on twitter as a content thief but I made fun of it instead of taking it seriously. I was given two copyright strikes on my channel. I did not know if a third was coming, which would get my channel terminated, so I had to act immediately. I abandoned all shame to beg for forgiveness in DMs, until I was shown mercy and the strikes were removed. I learned my lesson, but the damage was done. My channel was shadowbanned due to the copyright strikes and every video subsequent video got very few impressions. I would never reach my goal of channel monetization.

>*The heart of a demon never has regret even in death*

This will be the most I'll ever accomplish in the osu community, leaving a small impact for a short period of time, I don't think much of it and I am content with fading into obscurity. I played around with the idea of social climbing: Mass following 5000 people who liked my tweets, changing my profile picture for brand recognition, and cultivating a small fanbase. It was fun to see how far my name could spread. I removed all my followers and the people I followed throughout this period, iit is annoying to get DMs from hacked accounts, or have an FYP that is completely based on the combined interests of 5000 degenerates.

I have a new perspective on famous people. They are only people that make an effort to be seen. There are many more interesting, talented people in the world that choose to live in seclusion, their talents unrecognized by the public. Anyone can be a main character or a side character, it is their choice.
