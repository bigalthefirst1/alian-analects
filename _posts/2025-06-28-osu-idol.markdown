I’m not a clout chaser, but this was an opportunity too fun to ignore.

**Twitter**

>*High achievers are definitely lonely*

-Gu Zhen Ren, *Reverend Insanity*

Twitter has an osu!game tab that some people use as an alternative to the For You page. An FYP post will never end up on the osu!game tab, but something on the osu!game tab may end up on the FYP page. With this in mind, it's easy to gain traction by posting on the osu!game tab, since the first views will come from the target audience. I posted one of my YouTube osu meme edits and it gained 16k views and 300 likes. I felt like I discovered a secret method that no one else was using, since the average post in the osu!game tab was a mediocre osu clip with a few thousand views and 20 likes. The best way for a post to gain traction is for the poster to already have a following that consists of the target audience, but I had none of that, so this was my best option.

Getting engagements on a social media post has a distinct good feeling to it, like receiving praise for sharing a good idea, or making a joke that evokes laughter. Why did I stop posting? I was quite secluded during this period, so spending time alone let me fully immerse myself in your own interests. This was an interest that no one else cared about. I had no one to discuss my plans with, and I knew it would annoy them if I did. The headspace you need to be in as a Twitter poster is a positive feedback loop of attention seeking. Your sole purpose is to spark discussion and farm engagements for a synthetic feeling of social interaction. It requires a lot of free time, so once you find other things to do, it breaks the pattern.

**YouTube**

I posted some osu memes on my YouTube channel and they gained thousands of views. No one else was posting osu memes on YouTube and there was a vast amount of them on Twitter and Reddit that were being lost to time. YouTube is good at finding and resurfacing old videos. Downloading other people’s osu memes and reposting them without their permission is immoral, but I wanted to achieve a certain goal. I was close to reaching YouTube monetization because one of my videos from last year gained about 3000 watch hours, and you need 4000 in total within a year to be monetized. I knew I'd reach this goal if I stole memes and steadily uploaded them for the next two months.

**Downfall**

>*Not wanting to be trampled on, there are two ways. One is to become strong, strong until no one dares to step on you. Another is to turn into dog shit, something no one would want to step on.*

-Gu Zhen Ren, *Reverend Insanity*

Most people do not care if their memes get reposted on a different platform, but some people are more passionate, more sensitive than others, and there is nothing wrong with that. However, it is your fault if you overlook this. I temporarily applied a membership feature on an upcoming video where only channel members could watch it. I was testing a new feature I got for reaching a channel milestone. I was called out on twitter as a content thief but I made fun of it instead of taking it seriously. I was given two copyright strikes on my channel. I did not know if a third was coming, which would get my channel terminated, so I had to act immediately. I abandoned all shame and begged for forgiveness in DMs, until I was shown mercy and the strikes were removed. I learned my lesson, but the damage was done. My channel was shadowbanned due to the copyright strikes as every subsequent video got very few impressions. I would never reach my goal of channel monetization.

This will be the most I'll ever accomplish in the osu community, leaving a small impact for a short period of time, I don't think much of it and I am content with fading into obscurity, though I had fun seeing how far my name could spread.

I removed all my followers and the people I followed throughout this period, it's annoying to get DMs from hacked accounts, or have an FYP that is completely based on the combined interests of 5000 random people.

I have a new perspective on famous people. They are simply those who make an effort to be seen. There are much more interesting and talented people in the world that choose to live in seclusion, their talents unrecognized by the public. Anyone can be in the spotlight, it's their choice.
